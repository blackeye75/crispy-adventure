# Fretkey — Full Curriculum

Save as `docs/CURRICULUM.md`. This is the complete list of what the app teaches. It is the source for lesson writing in `content/` and for the concept IDs used by review.

This file covers theory. How to play it (strumming, lead and solos, fingerstyle, piano accompaniment, solo piano, musicianship) is in `docs/PLAYING.md`, with each stage tied back to the levels here.

## How to read this file

- **Core** lessons are instrument-agnostic theory, shown on both fretboard and keyboard.
- **Guitar** and **Piano** lessons apply the same idea on one instrument. A learner sees the strand for their chosen instrument and can open the other.
- **Ear** and **Rhythm** strands run alongside every level.
- Every lesson follows Understand → Listen → Learn → Practice and teaches one idea.
- Level 1 is free.

The owner reviews every lesson for accuracy before it is published. Claude Code must not publish theory content it has not verified against the reference tables at the end of this file.

## Level 1 — Foundations (free)

### 1.1 Sound and pitch (Core)
- What a note is; high and low
- The octave: same note, higher or lower
- How your instrument is laid out
- **Guitar:** strings, frets, string numbers, open strings E A D G B E, tuning
- **Piano:** white and black keys, the groups of two and three black keys, finding every C, middle C

### 1.2 The musical alphabet (Core)
- The seven natural notes A to G
- Sharps and flats
- Enharmonic notes (C# and Db are the same pitch)
- The 12-note chromatic scale
- Half steps and whole steps; why B–C and E–F have no note between them

### 1.3 Finding notes on your instrument
- **Guitar:** notes on the 6th and 5th strings · one fret = one half step · the 12th fret repeats · octave shapes to find any note · notes on all six strings
- **Piano:** naming every white key · naming black keys two ways · octave numbers (C4) · hand position and finger numbers 1–5

### 1.4 Rhythm I (Rhythm)
- Pulse and tempo (BPM)
- Bars and the 4/4 time signature
- Whole, half, quarter and eighth notes
- Rests
- Counting out loud: "1 and 2 and"

### 1.5 Reading music (Core, optional path)
- The staff, treble clef and bass clef
- Ledger lines and the grand staff
- **Guitar:** reading TAB and chord diagrams
- **Piano:** which hand reads which clef

### 1.6 Ear I (Ear)
- Higher or lower?
- Same or different?
- Singing back a note

## Level 2 — Building blocks

### 2.1 Intervals (Core)
- What an interval is: number and quality
- Seconds and thirds (major and minor)
- Perfect fourth and fifth; the tritone
- Sixths and sevenths; the octave
- Interval inversion (a third becomes a sixth)
- Compound intervals (9th, 11th, 13th)
- **Guitar:** interval shapes on adjacent strings; the G–B string shift
- **Piano:** counting half steps; interval shapes under the hand

### 2.2 The major scale (Core)
- The formula W–W–H–W–W–W–H
- Scale degrees 1 to 7 and their names (tonic, dominant, leading tone…)
- Building the major scale in any key with correct note spelling
- Solfege (Do Re Mi) and the link to Sa Re Ga
- **Guitar:** one-octave major scale shapes; playing along one string
- **Piano:** C, G, D, A, E fingering; then F, B and the flat keys

### 2.3 Keys and key signatures (Core)
- What "being in a key" means
- Order of sharps (F C G D A E B) and flats (B E A D G C F)
- The circle of fifths
- Relative major and minor
- Working out the key of a song

### 2.4 Rhythm II (Rhythm)
- Dotted notes and ties
- Sixteenth notes
- 3/4 and 6/8 time
- **Guitar:** down-up strumming and common strumming patterns
- **Piano:** hands together on simple rhythms

### 2.5 Ear II (Ear)
- Major or minor sound
- Recognising intervals going up
- Finding the tonic (home note) of a tune

## Level 3 — Harmony basics

### 3.1 Triads (Core)
- Stacking thirds
- Major, minor, diminished and augmented triads
- Suspended chords (sus2, sus4) and power chords
- Reading chord symbols

### 3.2 Chords of a key (Core)
- Harmonising the major scale: I ii iii IV V vi vii°
- Roman numerals and Nashville numbers
- Chord function: tonic, subdominant, dominant
- Why some chords "want" to move

### 3.3 Progressions (Core)
- I–IV–V
- I–V–vi–IV and vi–IV–I–V
- ii–V–I
- The 12-bar blues
- Cadences: authentic, plagal, half, deceptive
- Transposing a progression to any key
- **Guitar:** using a capo

### 3.4 Minor keys (Core)
- Natural minor formula W–H–W–W–H–W–W
- Relative minor versus parallel minor
- Harmonic minor and the major V chord
- Melodic minor
- Chords of a minor key

### 3.5 Inversions and slash chords (Core)
- Root position, first and second inversion
- Slash chords and bass lines
- **Guitar:** triad shapes on each set of three strings
- **Piano:** moving between chords with the least hand movement

### 3.6 Chords on your instrument
- **Guitar:** open chords · E-shape and A-shape barre chords · muting and clean changes
- **Piano:** block chords · broken chords and arpeggios · left-hand patterns (root–fifth, Alberti bass)

### 3.7 Ear III (Ear)
- Triad quality by ear
- Hearing I, IV and V
- Recognising common progressions

## Level 4 — Fluency on the instrument

### 4.1 Pentatonic and blues scales (Core)
- Major pentatonic (1 2 3 5 6)
- Minor pentatonic (1 b3 4 5 b7)
- The blues scale (adds b5)
- **Guitar:** the five pentatonic positions and how they connect
- **Piano:** pentatonic patterns in both hands

### 4.2 Owning the whole instrument
- **Guitar:** the CAGED system · five major scale positions · three-notes-per-string scales · linking positions along the neck · triads across the neck
- **Piano:** all 12 major and minor scales with fingering · arpeggios in all keys · shell and spread voicings · accompaniment patterns

### 4.3 Seventh chords (Core)
- maj7, dominant 7, m7, m7b5, dim7, m(maj7)
- Seventh chords of the major key
- The tritone inside the dominant 7 and how it resolves
- **Guitar:** movable seventh-chord shapes
- **Piano:** seventh-chord inversions

### 4.4 Chord tones and melody (Core)
- Targeting chord tones
- Passing tones and neighbour tones
- Guide tones (3rds and 7ths)
- Building a melody over a progression

### 4.5 Rhythm III (Rhythm)
- Triplets, swing and shuffle
- Syncopation
- Odd meters: 5/4 and 7/8

### 4.6 Expression
- **Guitar:** hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides, bends, vibrato
- **Piano:** dynamics, articulation, sustain pedal

### 4.7 Ear IV (Ear)
- Seventh-chord quality
- Pentatonic phrases by ear
- Descending and harmonic intervals

## Level 5 — Colour and movement

### 5.1 Modes (Core)
- The seven modes: Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, Locrian
- Relative approach (same notes, new home) versus parallel approach (same root, changed notes)
- The characteristic note of each mode
- Modal chord progressions
- **Guitar:** modes inside shapes you already know
- **Piano:** modes from each white key, then in all keys

### 5.2 Secondary dominants (Core)
- V of V and other secondary dominants
- Secondary leading-tone chords

### 5.3 Borrowed chords (Core)
- Modal interchange: iv, bIII, bVI, bVII in a major key
- The Picardy third
- The Neapolitan chord

### 5.4 Voice leading (Core)
- Common tones and stepwise motion
- Contrary motion
- Guide-tone lines
- **Guitar:** drop-2 voicings
- **Piano:** rootless voicings

### 5.5 Extended and altered chords (Core)
- 6, 6/9, add9
- 9th, 11th and 13th chords
- Altered dominants: b9, #9, #11, b13

### 5.6 Modulation (Core)
- Pivot-chord modulation
- Direct and common-tone modulation

### 5.7 Ear V (Ear)
- Recognising modes
- Hearing borrowed chords and secondary dominants

### Guitar and piano constants

- Standard guitar tuning, low to high: E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4. Adjacent strings are a perfect fourth apart, except G to B, which is a major third.
- Guitar range shown: open strings to fret 15 by default, up to fret 24 as an option.
- Middle C is C4. A4 = 440 Hz.
- Keyboard range shown with the fretboard: E2 to E6, which covers every note on a 24-fret guitar.
