# Fretkey — Playing Tracks (Technique and Musicianship)

Save as `docs/PLAYING.md`. `CURRICULUM.md` covers what music *is* (theory). This file covers how to *play* it: strumming, lead and solos, fingerstyle, the piano equivalents, and playing real music. Each track runs from basic to advanced, and every stage names the theory it uses so the learner always knows why something works.

## How playing tracks fit with theory

- The learner follows the theory path (Levels 1–6) and, alongside it, one or more playing tracks.
- Each stage below lists **Needs** (theory that must be done first) and **Uses** (theory applied in that stage). The app unlocks a stage when its "Needs" are complete and links back to those lessons.
- Lessons keep the same four steps. For technique lessons they mean:
  1. **Understand** — what the technique is and the theory behind it
  2. **Listen** — hear it slow, then at tempo
  3. **Learn** — watch it: close-up demo video plus animated tab or strum lane, synced to the fretboard or keyboard
  4. **Practice** — play along with a metronome or backing track, starting slow, tempo rising in steps
- Every stage ends with a **study piece**: a short original piece that uses everything in the stage.

**Songs and licensing:** do not put copyrighted songs, tabs or recordings in the app without a licence. Use original study pieces, original backing tracks and public-domain tunes. Teach "how to learn any song" as a skill instead.

# Guitar

## G1. Rhythm guitar and strumming

### Basic
*Needs: Level 1 (notes, Rhythm I). Uses: 4/4, quarter and eighth notes, open chords.*
- Holding the pick; relaxed wrist; where to strum
- Downstrokes on the beat
- Down-up eighth notes and the constant-motion rule (the hand keeps swinging, some swings miss the strings)
- First patterns: D D D D → D DU D DU → D DU UDU
- Changing chords in time; the "change early" trick
- Strumming only bass or only treble strings for texture
- Study piece: two- and three-chord songs in G, C, D, Em

### Intermediate
*Needs: Levels 2–3 (Rhythm II, triads, chords of a key). Uses: sixteenth notes, 3/4 and 6/8, syncopation, barre chords.*
- Sixteenth-note strumming
- Accents and dynamics: loud and soft inside one pattern
- Syncopated patterns and tied strums
- Left-hand and right-hand muting; percussive "chuck" strum
- Palm muting
- Patterns in 3/4 and 6/8
- Power-chord rhythm and riffs
- Barre-chord rhythm; moving one shape through a progression
- Shuffle and swing feel
- Off-beat (reggae/ska style) strumming
- Study piece: a full song form with verse and chorus patterns

### Advanced
*Needs: Levels 4–5 (seventh chords, inversions, Rhythm III). Uses: triads on string sets, extensions, odd meters, voice leading.*
- Funk sixteenth strumming with ghost strums and small chord fragments
- Chord embellishments: hammer-ons, sus and add9 decorations, fills between chords
- Mixing rhythm and lead: double-stop fills inside a rhythm part
- Arpeggiated rhythm parts with a pick
- Jazz comping with shell and drop-2 voicings
- Bossa nova and Latin patterns
- Odd-meter strumming (5/4, 7/8) and polyrhythm basics
- Writing your own rhythm part for a progression
- Study piece: a groove-based piece with two contrasting sections

## G2. Lead guitar and solos

### Basic
*Needs: Level 1, minor pentatonic intro from Level 4.1 (unlocked early for this track). Uses: minor pentatonic, the 12-bar blues, root notes.*
- Alternate picking on one string, then across strings
- Minor pentatonic, position 1
- Hammer-ons and pull-offs
- Slides
- First bends: half step and whole step, checked against the target note
- Vibrato
- First licks, and how each one sits on the scale
- Call and response phrasing; leaving space
- Landing on the root
- Study piece: a 12-bar blues solo with a backing track

### Intermediate
*Needs: Levels 3–4 (keys, chords of a key, pentatonics, chord tones). Uses: major and minor pentatonic, blues scale, major scale, arpeggios, relative major/minor.*
- All five pentatonic positions and moving between them
- The blues scale and the "blue note"
- Major pentatonic; switching between major and minor sounds
- Soloing with the full major and natural minor scales
- Targeting chord tones as the chords change
- Double stops
- Bend types: pre-bend, bend and release, unison bend
- Legato phrases
- Triad and seventh arpeggios in solos
- Sequences and patterns for building speed
- Shaping a solo: motif, repetition, build, peak, resolve
- Working out solos by ear
- Study piece: a melodic solo over a I–V–vi–IV progression

### Advanced
*Needs: Levels 5–6 (modes, secondary dominants, altered chords, jazz harmony). Uses: modes, harmonic and melodic minor, altered scale, chord-scale thinking.*
- Modal soloing: bringing out the characteristic note
- Playing the changes: a different scale or arpeggio per chord
- Harmonic minor and Phrygian dominant lines
- Arpeggio superimposition (playing one arpeggio over another chord)
- Altered and diminished sounds over dominant chords
- Economy picking and sweep picking
- Tapping
- Hybrid picking and string skipping
- Outside playing and resolving back in
- Time feel: playing behind and ahead of the beat
- Speed building with a method, not tension
- Study piece: a solo over changing keys

## G3. Fingerstyle

### Basic
*Needs: Level 1, open chords. Uses: chord tones, root notes, arpeggios.*
- Finger names p i m a; hand position; which finger owns which string
- Thumb on bass strings: playing chord roots
- First arpeggio patterns: p-i-m-a and p-i-m-i
- Picking through open chords in 4/4 and 3/4
- Pinches (thumb and finger together)
- Alternating thumb: root and fifth
- Study piece: an arpeggiated chord progression

### Intermediate
*Needs: Level 3 (inversions, slash chords, progressions). Uses: bass movement, inversions, melody over chords.*
- Travis picking: steady alternating bass with syncopated fingers
- A melody on top of an alternating bass
- Arpeggio patterns in 6/8
- Classical-style arpeggios and free strokes versus rest strokes
- Walking bass lines between chords
- Hammer-ons and pull-offs inside patterns
- Natural harmonics
- Alternate tunings (Drop D, DADGAD, open G, open D) and how the theory changes with them
- Study piece: a two-part piece with bass and melody

### Advanced
*Needs: Levels 4–5 (seventh chords, voice leading, extended chords). Uses: harmonising a melody, voice leading, reharmonisation.*
- Arranging a tune for solo guitar: bass, harmony and melody at once
- Chord melody
- Percussive techniques: thumb slap, body taps, string slaps
- Artificial and tapped harmonics
- Tremolo
- Two independent voices (counterpoint)
- Arranging any song in a suitable key and tuning
- Study piece: a complete solo arrangement

# Piano

## P1. Accompaniment and rhythm (the piano's "strumming")

### Basic
*Needs: Level 1, triads intro. Uses: 4/4, triads, chords of a key.*
- Block chords on the beat, right hand
- Left hand plays the root; then root and fifth
- Changing chords in time
- Broken chords
- First pop pattern: left-hand root, right-hand chord pulses
- Study piece: accompany a simple melody with three chords

### Intermediate
*Needs: Levels 2–3 (Rhythm II, inversions). Uses: inversions, syncopation, 3/4 and 6/8.*
- Inversions for smooth chord changes
- Left-hand patterns: root–fifth–octave, Alberti bass, arpeggiated tenths
- Ballad arpeggio patterns with pedal
- Syncopated pop and rock comping patterns
- Patterns in 3/4 and 6/8
- Fills between chords
- Study piece: a full song-form accompaniment

### Advanced
*Needs: Levels 4–5 (seventh chords, voice leading, extensions). Uses: shell and rootless voicings, walking bass, swing.*
- Jazz comping with shell and rootless voicings
- Walking bass in the left hand
- Stride basics
- Bossa nova and Latin patterns
- Gospel and R&B movement: passing chords and slides
- Odd meters
- Study piece: comping through a ii–V–I based tune

## P2. Melody, lead and solos

### Basic
*Needs: Level 1. Uses: major scale, pentatonic.*
- Five-finger position and even tone
- Playing simple melodies; thumb-under crossing
- Picking out a melody by ear
- First improvising on the pentatonic scale
- Call and response; leaving space

### Intermediate
*Needs: Levels 3–4. Uses: blues scale, chord tones, arpeggios.*
- Blues scale licks; grace notes and crushed notes
- Targeting chord tones over changes
- Runs and arpeggio fills
- Octaves and thirds/sixths for a fuller melody
- Shaping a solo: motif, build, peak, resolve
- Study piece: a 12-bar blues solo, left hand comping

### Advanced
*Needs: Levels 5–6. Uses: modes, altered scales, chord-scale thinking.*
- Modal improvising
- Playing the changes with scales and arpeggios per chord
- Approach notes, enclosures and bebop-style lines
- Altered and diminished sounds over dominants
- Outside playing and resolving
- Hands trading and locked-hands lines
- Study piece: a solo over a jazz progression

## P3. Solo piano (the piano's "fingerstyle")

### Basic
*Needs: Level 3.1–3.2. Uses: triads, chords of a key.*
- Melody in the right hand, single bass notes in the left
- Reading a lead sheet: melody plus chord symbols
- Simple left-hand chord under a melody

### Intermediate
*Needs: Level 3.5, Level 4.3. Uses: inversions, seventh chords, harmonising a melody.*
- Melody on top with chord tones beneath in the same hand
- Left-hand arpeggios under a melody
- Adding inner voices
- Using the pedal to connect harmony
- Study piece: a ballad arrangement

### Advanced
*Needs: Levels 5–6. Uses: voice leading, reharmonisation, extended chords.*
- Full arrangements with bass, harmony and melody
- Reharmonising a melody
- Changing texture across sections
- Rubato and expressive timing
- Stride and walking-bass solo styles
- Study piece: a complete solo arrangement of a public-domain tune

# Both instruments

## M1. Playing music and musicianship

### Basic
- Reading a chord chart; counting bars
- Playing with a metronome, then a backing track
- Starting and ending together with the track
- A daily practice routine: warm-up, new material, review, play for fun

### Intermediate
- Learning a song from start to finish: form, chords, rhythm, melody
- Working out chords by ear using the chords of the key
- Dynamics: making a chorus lift
- Transposing a song for a singer
- Recording yourself and listening back

### Advanced
- Playing with other musicians: roles, listening, leaving room
- Arranging: choosing parts for guitar and piano together
- Writing a song: progression, melody, form
- Performing: memorising, recovering from mistakes
- Finding your own style

# What this adds to the product

## New lesson blocks

| Block type | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `video` | Close-up demonstration (hands in frame), with slow-motion and loop |
| `tab` | Guitar tablature with rhythm, playable, synced to the fretboard |
| `strum-pattern` | Beat grid with down/up arrows, accents, mutes and ghost strums; playable |
| `picking-pattern` | Fingerstyle pattern showing string and finger (p i m a) per beat |
| `hands` | Piano part showing left and right hand with finger numbers, synced to the keyboard |
| `backing-track` | Original backing track in a chosen key and tempo, with chord display |

All are defined as data and rendered and played by the app, except `video`, which is recorded.

## Canvas additions (see `CANVAS.md`, "Technique lane")

A single lane under the instruments that shows time: strum arrows, tab, picking pattern or piano hands, with a moving playhead in sync with the audio and the instrument highlights.

## Practice tools

- Tempo trainer: starts slow and raises the tempo each time a passage is played cleanly
- Loop any section of a tab, pattern or video
- Count-in and metronome accents

## Honest note on workload

Technique cannot be taught by text alone. Each technique lesson needs a short demo video of real hands, which the owner records. Plan about 5–10 minutes of footage per stage, filmed in batches. Guitar footage can come from the owner; piano footage needs a capable pianist.
